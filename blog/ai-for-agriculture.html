AI for Agriculture — What Actually Works in the Field

Notes from building Plant Assistant for small-scale farmers.

AI/MLPWAAgriculture

The premise

Farmers often lack quick, reliable plant information. Existing solutions are expensive, require constant internet, or aren't designed for real-world field conditions. Plant Assistant was my attempt at something simple, visual, and resilient.

The uncomfortable lesson: ML is the easy part

Everyone wants to talk about the model. Fine-tuning an image classifier to identify regional plant species is not the hard part. The hard parts were:

  • Data. Getting labeled images of plants that actually grow in the target region. Public datasets are Western-biased.
  • Connectivity. Running inference without internet, on cheap Android phones, without draining the battery.
  • Trust. Farmers trust other farmers. Not apps. Not AI. The product had to earn that trust through accuracy and second opinions.
  • Context. "This plant has blight" is useless without "…and here's what to do this week, in your climate, given your local pesticide availability."

Design decisions that mattered

  • Offline-first PWA. Service workers cache the last-known advisory. The app works with zero bars.
  • Geolocation-based advisory. Season, rainfall, and local pesticide recommendations come from location, not the ML model.
  • Chatbot for follow-ups. After identification, users can ask "what if I already sprayed X?" — natural language cuts friction.
  • Small models, quantized. 8-bit quantized MobileNet variants on-device for the common cases; cloud inference for edge cases when online.

What I got wrong

The first prototype leaned heavily on Streamlit for iteration speed. Great for demos. Terrible for mobile. Streamlit's client-server model fights everything a PWA is trying to do — offline caching, instant interaction, installability.

If I rebuilt it today, I'd separate the ML inference API from the UI from day one. Lean frontend, thin API. The Streamlit version stays as an internal testing tool.

The thesis going forward

AI in agriculture doesn't need to be cutting-edge. It needs to be present, offline-capable, and locally contextualized. That's a systems and product problem more than a model problem. The teams that win here will be the ones that treat the field as the primary environment, not the lab.
